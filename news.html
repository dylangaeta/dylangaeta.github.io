Milestone

Defended my dissertation

  • Defended U.S. emissions of trace gas air pollutants inferred from a long-term atmospheric measurement network: from fumigants to fossil fuels.
  • Three case studies, each using NOAA GML flask measurements and a geostatistical inverse model to map U.S. emissions, trends, and uncertainties:
  • Sulfuryl fluoride: California dominates U.S. emissions, mostly from structural fumigation of western drywood termites.
  • Methyl bromide: emissions fell after the Montreal Protocol phase-out but not to zero, sustained by quarantine and pre-shipment uses and California strawberry growing.
  • Propane, butane, and pentane: oil and gas basins dominate, led by the Permian, Anadarko, Eagle Ford, and Haynesville, with implications for ozone downwind.
  • Common thread: large discrepancies between top-down and bottom-up estimates.

Advisor: Prof. Scot M. Miller  ·  Department of Environmental Health & Engineering, Johns Hopkins University

Paper

First first-author paper: sulfuryl fluoride

  • Published California dominates U.S. emissions of the pesticide and potent greenhouse gas sulfuryl fluoride in Communications Earth & Environment.
  • Using NOAA GGGRN measurements and a geostatistical inverse model, we found California emits 60–85% of U.S. SO₂F₂ (0.26 ± 0.10 Gg/yr), equal to 5.5–12% of global emissions.
  • Coordinated an embargoed press release with the journal and the JHU Hub; the paper went live on April 3.
  • Fielded interview requests from the Los Angeles Times, Inside Climate News, and Southern California Public Radio, and recorded audio for Sea Change Radio.
  • Coverage followed in The Guardian, Scientific American, The Hill, the San Francisco Chronicle, and Courthouse News (all media).

D C Gaeta, J Mühle, I J Vimont, M Crotwell, L Hu, J B Miller, K McKain, B C Baier, M Zhang, J Bao, B R Miller, S M Miller  ·  Commun. Earth Environ. 5, 161
